Official, from M-VAVE

M-VAVE's own tool for its open firmware: official, not one of the open projects below.

M-VAVE FM1 Workbench

by M-VAVE
official · M-VAVEFM-1

M-VAVE's own tool in the browser (English or Chinese), three in one. An editor: projects, sounds and samples of FM1 Open. A flasher: it installs FM1 Open on the FM-1 and switches back to the official V15. A preview: FM1 Open plays in the browser without an FM-1. It also hands out the development kit ("Source + SDK") to build your own on FM1 Open.

Official, and not open source as a whole: the app is GPL-3.0 with an SDK exception, the sound engine a closed library (binary SDK). FM1 Open has a card on the hub; it installs from here, not from the hub.

fm1-emu

by Joakim Eriksson
FM-1SMK-37

The whole FM-1 in your browser: front panel, screen and sound, running the firmwares' own update packages unchanged. Most of the hub's firmwares are one click away; the SMK-37 too.

fm1-emulator

by Simon Johansson
FM-1

Emulates the FM-1's chip and board on QEMU and runs the same .fwsc package you would install, with the screen, keys, knobs and sound. A desktop app for macOS (Apple Silicon) and Linux (x86-64).

Editors & sounds

Edit voices, manage banks, bring your own samples.

FM1 Editor & Librarian

by Benny Sparra
FM-1

A DX7 voice editor and patch librarian in the browser: banks, every DX7 parameter and the FM-1's effects, sent over USB MIDI. For the stock firmware and FM-1+VA.

FM-1

The DX7 as a free plugin and app: edit voices and banks and send them as SysEx. The stock FM-1 takes DX7 SysEx.

zp12 sample editor + backup

by zvenson
FM-1

Your own samples on the zp12 firmware: drop a WAV, trim it or chop a loop, hear it as the FM-1 plays it, put it on a pad. And a backup of zp12's loops, sounds, songs and samples.

FM-1

FM-1 Workbench (community)

by thegiantsnail
FM-1

A community workbench in the browser: DX7 voice library and editor, a drum sequencer, a MIDI file player and a speech tab, with a software FM-1 when none is connected. Also an Android app and a VST3 / CLAP controller. Not M-VAVE's FM1 Workbench.

Apps

Programs and phone apps that work beside your FM-1: its screen big on your computer, a firmware's panel on your FM-1.

FM-1 Mirror

by Patrick F. Kellogg
FM-1

Shows the FM-1's own screen live on your computer or phone, as big as you like: sharp, in colour or in high-contrast yellow on black. Made by a player who does not see well. Its Big Screen firmware is M-VAVE's Official Open firmware with one small addition; M-VAVE's Workbench puts the official one back any time.

Mac, Windows and Linux; the Android app is new and not yet tested by its maker.

Download ↗Watch the video ↗Source ↗
Licence: GPL-3.0 (the app), MIT (the screen-sending part, for any firmware)

RemoteFM1

by Quixotic7
FM-1

With the RemoteFM1 apps (remotefm1.com, iOS, Android) the FM-1's keys, knobs and screen play a firmware running in the app: any firmware on the hub in the browser, over 80 in the iOS app, no reflashing. On its own it is a 24-bit DX7 with Yamaha's ROM voices and 16 user cartridges, and a USB, Bluetooth and TRS MIDI controller.

Open ↗Install it ↗Source ↗
Licence: MPL-2.0

Backup & rescue

Save the whole flash before you experiment, and bring back an FM-1 that no installer reaches. For Windows, macOS and Linux.

Your FM-1 does not start, update or save any more? Find what you see, and do only that step.

M-Flasher

by jvitkauskas
FM-1SMK-37

An app for Windows, macOS and Linux: switches a running FM-1 into the chip's USB download mode with one button, saves the whole flash, then installs a .fwsc. Works when the firmware is broken, and on the newer flash chip.

Releases ↗How to use ↗Source ↗
Licence: MIT

MvaveFM1Unbricker

by Quixotic7
FM-1

Unbricks an FM-1: first a full flash backup (read twice and compared), then it writes the firmware and checks every byte. Both flash chips. Windows and Linux; macOS through the FM-1 Transporter.

Source on GitHub ↗
Licence: MIT

FM1 Recovery

by SikkenWorks
FM-1

A guided recovery app for Windows and, new, Android (the phone on USB): puts M-VAVE's V15 back through the FM-1's download mode – Simple Mode walks you through it, a verified full-flash backup first, then it writes and verifies. Built on Quixotic7's MvaveFM1Unbricker.

Releases (Windows .exe, Android .apk) ↗How to use ↗Source ↗

Melodee's fm1-recover

by keremimo
FM-1

A command-line recovery for an FM-1 in download mode, no Python needed: a full flash backup first, then it writes and verifies. Windows, macOS and Linux. Its releases up to 1.6.1 refuse the newer flash chip; a test branch takes it (see From a Mac).

How to use ↗Source ↗
Licence: GPL-3.0

The hub's Rescue

by zvenson
FM-1

Puts a firmware from the hub back on an FM-1 that no installer reaches any more, through the chip's own bootloader: it checks the chip and the package first and writes only the firmware area. Both flash chips. A Python script for Linux.

How to rescueSource
Licence: GPL-3.0

FM-1 Transporter

by kurogedelic
FM-1

Hardware: a Seeed XIAO RP2040 wired to the FM-1's USB lines reads and writes its whole flash, 1 MiB in about three seconds.

Source on GitHub ↗
Licence: MIT

Hardware

Little boxes you build for the FM-1: no computer needed.

fm1buddy

by konsumer
FM-1

A USB MIDI host box: plug USB MIDI keyboards and controllers straight into the FM-1. With a small screen it is also a firmware switcher: pick a firmware from its SD card and it installs it on the FM-1, and it maps MIDI on the way. Ready-made parts (Adafruit Feather RP2040 USB Host or a Raspberry Pi), installed from the browser.

Build and install it ↗Source ↗
Licence: GPL-3.0

For makers

Build your own firmware, port one, or look under the hood.

Build your own

by zvenson and konsumer and hugelton
FM-1

Two templates to start from: Dinghy, the smallest FM-1 firmware to build on, with the plumbing done and documented, and foma, Dinghy made smaller: one C file, nothing to install but Docker, a browser emulator that reloads as you save, CI builds and a web preview. Felucca's BUILDING.md shows how a full firmware is built, and felucca-bsp is the chip and board layer under it.

Dinghy ↗foma ↗Felucca BUILDING.md ↗felucca-bsp ↗
Licence: GPL-3.0 · felucca-bsp MPL-2.0

fm1-to-smk37

by genkigenki
FM-1SMK-37

A kit to port an FM-1 firmware to the SMK-37 with a coding agent such as Claude Code: an agent playbook, a setup file for what the pads, faders, outer keys and wheels do, mapping patterns, examples and pitfalls. The FM-1 build stays byte-identical, everything SMK behind one switch, ready to send upstream.

The kit's website ↗Agent playbook ↗Source ↗
Licence: CC BY-NC 4.0 (documentation, non-commercial licence)

FM-1

FM-1-RE: the reverse engineering of the FM-1's firmware and its update protocol. jl-uboot-tool: a flasher and dumper for JieLi chips like the FM-1's.
