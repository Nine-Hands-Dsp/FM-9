VIBE FM · THE PLATFORM EARLY ACCESS

One Vibe.
Every device.

VIBE FM is a free, open platform for instruments. One file – a Vibe – plays in your browser, in your DAW and on every device that joins in. The same instrument, the same sound, wherever you are. And the FM-1's firmwares play along – every one on the hub, converted and unchanged.

Built by all of us: the people who write these instruments, the people who play them, and the people who build the boxes they run on. That means you, too.

Where we are. Honestly.

The software is here: the Playground runs, the Lab is open, the plugin is already running on thousands of computers worldwide. The VIBE FM Mk1 is in the making – it's a working prototype atm, and we've just ordered the first circuit boards. It's not a product you can buy yet.

And we're not alone any more. Since a few weeks the big names are stamping their hooves: open firmware is suddenly on every roadmap. Let's be clear: a small idea developer cannot match their factories, their prices or their marketing.

We're going to try anyway. Because the idea is bigger than one box: not another device with open firmware, but one standard that runs on all of them – theirs included, if they let it. That part no factory can build. That part is us.

A crowdfunding for the VIBE FM Mk1 starts soon. If you want the original – the box this was all made for – that's where it happens. Everything else stays free, open, and runs on whatever you already have.

OberVave 3

by zvenson · 3.9 · native

Four tracks of analog-style poly – two oscillators a voice, a 12 / 24 dB filter, an arpeggiator, a step sequencer per track, a mixer with drive – and any track can be an audio track. In your DAW: every sound parameter automatable, an Editor of its own.

New in 3.9

LOAD and SAVE pages (F3 / F4): PRESET – one sound as a file; PATTERN – the pattern with its sounds, mixer and audio tracks, the takes as WAVs beside it, loaded into any pattern; AUDIO – a take as a WAV, and a take another pattern plays, or any WAV, into any pattern and track; SONG (ALL) – everything, and NEW SONG empties all 64 patterns (settings and programs stay). F5 is STORE, the program slot.

3.8.1 – GAIN for IN 1 / IN 2 on the AUDIO page (+0 … 40 dB) and an input level bar top right, red when it clips – turn the interface down below clipping and make the level up here. A take keeps one latency from its start.

3.8 – An audio track belongs to its pattern: each pattern its own audio tracks and takes – they change with it on the bar, and all takes share ~23 min of stereo. A filter per audio track (LP / HP / BP, cutoff, resonance, drive). CUT in sixteenths, bars or milliseconds. UNDO with 16 steps: hold RETURN, turn encoder 1.

3.7 – Audio tracks: any of the four tracks plays audio instead of the synth (SOUND, last page: TYPE). Record from IN 1 / IN 2 – mono, or both as stereo – or resample what you hear; as a loop or as a tape from bar 1 for a whole song; overdub; cut (erase, delete, crop). Up to ~5:49 of stereo or ~11:39 of mono a track; the takes are WAVs on the card. 64 patterns (was 8). Songs: FILES (F6 three times) saves all 64 patterns, the sounds, the mixers, the settings and the audio as one named song and loads it back – and loads any WAV from the card or a USB stick into a track (other rates converted). It needs 320 MB of memory: a phone's browser may say no.

Since 3.3: LP 24 is a real four-pole ladder now, the resonance fed back around all four stages with a tanh in the loop, so it compresses instead of thinning and the bass stays. No hard clipping anywhere in a voice, DRIVE from warm to fuzz, VINTAGE wanders on the cutoff too. Since 3.2: every pattern keeps its own tempo, VOICE sits with the sound under SOUND after OSC 2; since 3.1: the sequencer follows the song position and your loops, the tracks play out over MIDI with clock.

VIBE DJ

by zvenson · 2.5 · native · two decks

Playing a few tunes on stage before the gig, mixing a little? VIBE DJ turns your groovebox into a DJ deck in a moment, no extra cables. Two decks with SYNC, pitch, a crossfader, DJ EQ and filter, and the next track in your headphones. Try it with the tracks of the FM-1 Jukebox.

ZP12

by zvenson · 3.0 · native · 12-bit sampling drums

The sound of the 80s 12-bit samplers: 32 sounds pitched by the playback rate, no interpolation, the grit of 12 bits, eight channels with their filters. Sample from a mic, the line-in or your computer over USB, trim it, play it, build loops and songs. 12 or 16 bit, as much sample time as you choose. The same engine as zp12 on the FM-1.

HALO

by zvenson · 0.2 · native · a room that plays itself

HALO has no performer. It has a microphone and a room. Whoever walks through plays it – without knowing: a step strikes a bell, a conversation turns into a choir in key, a hummed note moves the harmony. When the room is empty, it keeps singing what it heard last. Four engines – strings, grain clouds, a choir, bells – in a vast shimmering space. For lobbies, galleries, shop windows, stairwells: microphone in, speakers out, leave it.

Build your own ↑

And your beloved FM-1 firmwares? They run on VIBE FM too – converted from their source, unchanged: in the VST plugin today, on the VIBE FM hardware tomorrow.

Native or converted?

The VST plugin plays both. A converted FM-1 firmware is the FM-1's, unchanged; a native one is written for VIBE FM.

Converted FM-1 firmwareNative VIBE FM
Screenthe FM-1's 240 × 240the full 480 × 320
Controlsthe FM-1's 7 encoders, 14 buttons, 27 keysevery VIBE control: pads, four knobs, A / B / DATA, labelled keys
In your DAW7 generic knobs; the tempo, start and stopnamed parameters, the song position and loops
Enginethe FM-1's, unchanged – a little extra CPU for the emulated chipwritten for VIBE FM – the lowest latency
